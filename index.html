    FINAL DEBRIEF

    When did your judgment change?

    A scientifically promising intervention can still violate research ethics if the people carrying its risks cannot make an adequately informed decision.

    01

    Autonomy

    Consent requires material information, not just a signature.

    02

    Beneficence

    Protecting infants from measles was a legitimate scientific and public health goal.

    03

    Non-maleficence

    Emerging safety signals demand review even when the mechanism is uncertain.

    04

    Justice

    Communities facing disproportionate risk also deserve meaningful protections and participation.

    The final question

    You direct the health department. You learn that a scientifically justified study failed to disclose material information to families. What are your first three actions—and how would you explain them in public?

    Historical clarification

    Parents signed consent forms and knew their children were in a vaccine study. The central consent failure was inadequate disclosure that the Edmonston–Zagreb vaccine was experimental and unlicensed in the United States. An international mortality signal in some high-potency vaccine groups prompted action; that finding should not be presented as proof of deaths caused by the Los Angeles study. Historical accounts report no documented vaccine-associated injury in the Los Angeles cohort.
